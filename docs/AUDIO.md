# Audio

How every sound in this game is made. Adopted from `RubenTipparach/tom-lander`
`audio.md`, read with the owner's permission (2026-09-04) and recorded here
rather than copied: that repository scores a 152 BPM chip-adjacent game in C
dorian, and this one is a museum at night. What carries over is the
discipline and one of its two pipelines. What is ours is the music itself.

Governed by CLAUDE.md 5 (every asset is a static file with an editable
source, generated by a committed script) and CLAUDE.md 7.

---

## 1. Two pipelines, one per kind, and the split is the point

**A score is instruments playing notes. An effect is an object making a
noise.** One tool is right for each and neither is right for both, so there
are two renderers and exactly two: adding a third way to make a sound is the
divergent path CLAUDE.md 4.1 forbids.

| | Score | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| source, committed | `assets/audio/music/*.mid` | `assets/audio/sfx/*.csd` and `museum.orc` |
| rendered by | fluidsynth against FluidR3_GM | csound |
| why | a soundfont IS an orchestra, and the score wants one | modal resonator banks, friction inside a band: a struck body, not a played note |
| opens in | LMMS, Ardour, MuseScore | any text editor, and `csound door_open.csd` |
| written by | `tools/gen_music.py` | `tools/gen_sfx.py` |

```
data/world/music.json     the world's scale, its six word pitches, its motifs
        |
        +-- tools/gen_music.py --> *.mid --> fluidsynth --+
        |                                                 |
        +-- tools/gen_sfx.py ----> *.csd --> csound ------+
                                                          |
                                        tools/audio_render.py: sox masters both
                                        (no dither, DC block, trim, fade, level)
                                                          |
                                   assets/audio/**  committed products
                                                          |
                                   tools/check_audio.py   the gate
```

**Why not a soundfont for the effects.** It was tried and shipped. A wood
block through General MIDI sounds like a wood block being PLAYED, because
that is what a sampled instrument is: the strike is a musician's, the tuning
is a note, and the room is a concert hall. A fingertip on a museum label is
none of those. Section 5 has the whole account.

**Why not sfxr.** Also tried, and installed: `jsfxr` is a dependency and can
be used where a retro blip is genuinely wanted. Everything it makes carries
an arcade voice, which is exactly wrong for a liminal building.

```sh
./scripts/install-audio-tools.sh   # csound, fluidsynth, the soundfont, sox, oggenc
./scripts/render-audio.sh          # both generators, then the gate
./scripts/render-audio.sh --check  # nothing has drifted from its source
python3 tools/check_audio.py       # the gate alone, on what is committed
cd assets/audio/sfx && csound door_open.csd    # one effect, by hand
```

**The score's half is tom-lander's soundfont path, not its Strudel path.**
That repo has two: a Node synth driven by Strudel patterns, and an orchestral
route through MIDI to fluidsynth or LMMS. The Strudel route was tried here
first and is gone, with the reason in section 5.

**LMMS is installed and is the second seat, not a second pipeline.** The same
`.mid` opens in it (`xvfb-run -a lmms --allowroot render out.mmp -o out.wav
-f wav -s 44100` renders a project headless, per tom-lander's own notes), so
a sound that wants a real synth or a real effect chain is authored there
against the same source. It has never yet been needed.

## 2. The musical world

**The Elmorians count in sixes, so the music does.** Six notes, six beat
bars, and phrases of six. `data/world/music.json` is the authority and every
reader asks it (CLAUDE.md 6): the generators read it, and `check_audio.py`
refuses a score with a note outside it.

**The scale is D natural minor with the second degree missing.**

```
D  F  G  A  Bb  C          six notes, one semitone
                ^^^^
                A to Bb, the only semitone in the scale
```

Two consequences, and they are the whole character of this music. There is
**no leading tone**, so nothing is ever pulled home and a phrase can stop
anywhere. And there is **exactly one semitone**, between the fifth and the
sixth, so every moment of unease in the score is the same interval returning:
scarce, and therefore heard.

**The six words of the lexicon are the six notes.** The speech room's pads
are cut to them, so a phrase in that room is a phrase of music and the score
quotes the same notes:

| # | word | note | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | light | A4 | the open fifth, the brightest thing they have |
| 1 | stone | D3 | the root, an octave down: the ground |
| 2 | eye | C5 | the seventh, high and unresolved, because an eye is a question |
| 3 | touch | G4 | the plainest interval, and touch is how they speak at all |
| 4 | door | F4 | the third, the only note that says minor out loud |
| 5 | dark | Bb4 | the one semitone. The word that was only ever written |

### The motifs

| name | notes | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **hall_six** | A C A G F D | the house theme. Six notes, a falling arc that touches the seventh and walks home, arriving without ever being pulled |
| **the_gaze** | A G D | three notes for three eyes, stated by three voices at three registers, entering one after another |
| **going_to_stone** | C Bb A G F (Bb) | the descent of someone darkening. The sixth returns at the end and is HELD instead of falling: the piece stops on the semitone |
| **the_greeting** | A C G F | light, eye, touch, door. Pads 0, 2, 3, 4 |
| **the_farewell** | D Bb G C | stone, dark, touch, eye. Pads 1, 5, 3, 2. Not the greeting reversed, and it leaves on the seventh |

**In the manner of Riven, not in imitation of it.** What is taken is the
approach: slow, modal, more silence than sound, a small number of cells
restated in other registers rather than developed, and an ending that
withholds. Nothing of that score is reproduced, as nothing of any real
culture is (CLAUDE.md 6).

### The score

`assets/audio/music/hall_six.mid` is 28 bars of six beats at 48 BPM, about
three and three quarter minutes, and it loops. Tubular bells carry the
theme, a bowed pad the drone, kalimba the gaze, choir the ancestor, and a
timpani strikes three times in the whole piece, the last the quietest.

## 2a. The instrument set

`assets/audio/sfx/museum.orc` is the one place a body is described, and every
effect is struck out of it. Change a body there and every sound made of it
changes.

| body | what it is | how it is built |
|---|---|---|
| `stone` | cut stone: dense, dead within a breath | five inharmonic modes, the high ones dying first |
| `singing` | a stone cut to ring: the speech pads | four nearly harmonic modes, a long first one, and an audible strike |
| `bronze` | a bell | a bell's own ratios, 1 : 2.76 : 5.40 : 8.93, ringing for seconds |
| `label` | a nail across card over board | a band of noise at 2.6 kHz through two very dead modes |
| `slab` | stone across stone | friction whose band wanders and catches, over the slab's own body |
| `seat` | a heavy stone landing | three low modes and the grit the impact presses out |
| `carpet` | a shoe on museum carpet | a soft low thump and a brush of pile |
| `air` | the hall at night | very low noise through four narrow resonances, each drifting |

**Decay is written in seconds, not as a Q.** A resonator wants a Q and a
person wants a time, so the orchestra converts: for a mode at f ringing for
t seconds, `Q = pi * f * t`. Every score therefore reads in the units the
sound is designed in.

**The orchestra is seeded.** `seed 6` at the top, because every one of these
has friction or grit in it and csound otherwise seeds its noise from the
clock: two renders of an unchanged source would differ and `--check` could
not tell a changed sound from a re-rendered one.

## 3. Sound design rules

- **A sound is an object, not an instrument.** The body is chosen for what the
  thing IS, and it is struck rather than played: a nail on card for a
  fingertip, cut stone for a disc turning, a slab for a slab.
- **No noise beds.** Noise appears only where a real object makes it, band
  limited, under a tonal body. A slab grinding across a floor may hiss; a
  room may not.
- **Everything must survive a phone speaker.** Below about 200 Hz a phone
  makes almost no sound, so every effect carries a knock in the mids as well
  as its weight underneath, and the score keeps nothing below 80 Hz at all.
- **Levels are authored, never trimmed at runtime.** A plaque tick is a small
  sound and a door is a big one because they are rendered that way.
- **A bed loops, and its seam is cross faded.** A loop that clicks is worse
  than no bed.

## 4. The gate

`tools/check_audio.py`, pure stdlib so it runs wherever the build does. It
measures the three things that went wrong:

- **flatness**, the spectral flatness measure: the geometric mean of the
  power spectrum over its arithmetic mean. White noise is 1, a struck body is
  near 0. This is literally "how much like static is it", and every sound
  here now measures 0.001 or less.
- **register**, the share of energy above 200 Hz (what a phone can play) and
  below 80 Hz (what nothing can).
- **level**, peak and RMS, so a sound is neither inaudible nor shouting.
- **the scale**, for a score: every note against `data/world/music.json`.

## 5. What went wrong the first time, and why it is written down

The first audio shipped and the owner heard it as "random static, awful". It
was, and each cause is worth keeping:

- **Everything was hand rolled noise.** Eighteen effects synthesised in
  Python from filtered white noise, following the shape of tom-lander's
  weapon generator without noticing that a whoosh and a museum are opposite
  problems. Measured afterwards: the room tone had 46% of its energy spread
  broadband above 320 Hz. That is the definition of hiss, and it played
  under everything.
- **The score lived where nobody could hear it.** 96% of its energy sat
  below 320 Hz and 0.9% above 640: on a phone, a vague hum. The drone was
  written at D1, 37 Hz.
- **The renderer left a DC offset.** Several FluidR3 samples carry one and
  the first soundfont render came off at +0.028: headroom spent on a
  constant, a thump at every start and stop, and, measured as a spectrum,
  nearly half the energy in a band nothing reproduces. A `highpass 20` in the
  sox chain is the fix and it belongs in mastering, not in a generator.
- **Then the effects were instruments.** The soundfont fixed the static and
  introduced the next problem: General MIDI is a set of instruments, so a
  wood block sounded like a wood block being played. The owner heard it at
  once ("why are sfx piano?"). Effects moved to csound; the score stayed,
  because for a score being an orchestra is the point.
- **Three smaller things the move surfaced.** sox dithers when it narrows
  float to 16 bits, and dither is random, so `--check` reported drift on all
  eighteen files until `-D` turned it off. Trimming before normalising judged
  a quiet float signal against a share of full scale and deleted the smallest
  effects entirely. And the word `stone` sat at D3, an octave under the other
  five, so the farewell, which OPENS on it, opened on a note no phone can
  reproduce: it is D4 now, still the lowest of the six.
- **The room tone did not loop.** Godot's wav importer defaults to
  `loop_mode=0`, so the bed played once for eight seconds and then the hall
  was silent for the rest of the session. The setting is per file and lives
  in the committed `.import` sidecar.
- **Nothing measured any of it.** Every suite was green throughout. The gate
  in section 4 exists because the owner's ears were the only test that
  existed, and that is a test that only runs after a release.

The rule the whole section comes down to: **CLAUDE.md 5 already said to use a
real tool driven headlessly, the way Blender makes the models.** Audio was
the one asset class where that was quietly skipped, and it was the one that
shipped broken.
